Star projects

Atombeat · 2026– · peptides, radioconjugates

Agent-run computational chemistry

Structure preparation and FEP map setup ate three to five days of a scientist's attention every design cycle. I built the agent layer on the Claude Agent SDK that exposes our prep, docking, MD, FEP and analysis tools as callable services over MCP; agents resolve protonation and tautomers, build and equilibrate systems, construct the perturbation maps and submit the jobs, then hand back a package. The scientist steers and adjudicates.

The setup phase now finishes in one unattended overnight run, under 12 hours. It carries the peptide program's computational work day to day.

Client program · 2025– · oncology, radioligand therapy

Radioconjugate lead optimization, end to end

A U.S. pharmaceutical partner needed a peptide radioconjugate against a transmembrane GPCR optimized for potency and tumor uptake, delivered on a schedule. I am the program's sole CADD scientist and its project manager: FEP-driven residue-by-residue SAR, ranked design packages with structural rationale, and the technical conversation with the partner. I presented the technical case and negotiated terms alongside our BD team.

Became Atombeat's first commercial contract in North America. FEP has held to a 0.85 kcal/mol mean absolute error across about fifty peptides; validated candidates are in tumor-uptake follow-up.

UC Irvine · 2019 · GPCRs, small molecules

Membrane-protein binding free energies, shipped in AmberTools

MM/PBSA treated a cell membrane as one uniform slab, so binding predictions for GPCRs were rough guesses, and the Poisson–Boltzmann solve underneath was too slow to run at scale. I ran the simulations that parameterized a depth-dependent dielectric membrane and wrote the GPU matrix module for the solver.

Correlation with measured affinities went from R = 0.59 to 0.91; the solver got about 65% faster. Released in AmberTools 2019, carried into gmx_MMPBSA, and since used by other groups on a cannabinoid GPCR and a bacterial efflux pump, and cited by drug-discovery companies and the National Cancer Institute.

Boehringer Ingelheim · 2024 · process chemistry

Catalyst selectivity models chemists run themselves

Choosing the catalyst and ligand for an API-forming reaction was trial and error, three to four weeks of experimental screening per route. I built a fully automatic pipeline that predicts selectivity from DFT-derived descriptors across three metal catalysts and two ligand families, with a classifier that flags ligands giving perfect regioselectivity at about 90% precision.

Routes start from a model-ranked shortlist instead of a screen. I handed the pipeline to the senior synthetic chemists, and they run it on their own programs.

Working with partner programs

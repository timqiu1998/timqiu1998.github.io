In production

Interactive automation of the peptide program (Atombeat, 2026–)

I lead the peptide discovery program and the effort to automate it with a scientist in the loop. I built the agent layer, on the Claude Agent SDK, that wraps our structure preparation, docking, MD, FEP and analysis tools as typed, agent-callable services over MCP and custom tool APIs. Agents resolve protonation and tautomer states, build and equilibrate systems, construct perturbation maps and submit jobs, then hand back a package; the scientist steers, adjudicates the flagged edges and poses, and decides what goes to synthesis. It took the setup phase of a DMTA cycle from three to five days of a scientist's attention to a single overnight run.

Client lead optimization, radioconjugate against a transmembrane GPCR (2025–)

I lead and project-manage the program end to end: FEP-driven optimization of peptide potency and tumor uptake, residue-by-residue SAR, and direct technical communication with the partner. Across about fifty peptides the FEP predictions have held to a 0.85 kcal/mol mean absolute error against experiment, which is on par with good small-molecule benchmarks on a modality where FEP usually does worse. Several validated candidates have moved to tumor-uptake follow-up. I presented the technical case for this collaboration to the partner and negotiated terms alongside our business-development team; it became Atombeat's first commercial contract in North America.

Cyclic-peptide design platform (Atombeat)

Co-developed with the team: an active-learning loop for orally accessible cyclic peptides that pulls in non-natural amino acids for permeability and stability, and optimizes predicted affinity across several objectives at once. Pilot campaigns returned experimental hit rates of about ten percent of synthesized designs.

De novo binder pipeline (Atombeat)

A parallelized RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN pipeline that produced more than a hundred structurally diverse binder candidates for an inflammatory bowel disease target; multiple designs are in experimental validation.

Adopted by others

Implicit-membrane MM/PBSA and GPU Poisson–Boltzmann (UC Irvine, 2019)

The depth-dependent dielectric membrane model raised the correlation between predicted and measured binding affinities for membrane targets from R = 0.59 to 0.91; the GPU matrix module gave the solver about a 65% efficiency gain. Both were released in AmberTools 2019. The membrane model was carried into gmx_MMPBSA and has since been used by independent groups on a cannabinoid GPCR and a bacterial efflux pump, and cited by drug-discovery companies and the National Cancer Institute.

Catalyst-selectivity models for API synthesis (Boehringer Ingelheim, 2024)

A fully automatic pipeline that predicts selectivity for API-forming reactions across three metal catalysts and two ligand families from DFT-derived descriptors, with a classifier that flags ligands giving perfect regioselectivity at about 90% precision. It replaced an estimated three to four weeks of experimental ligand screening per route, and I handed it to the senior synthetic chemists to run on their own programs.

Expert-LLM ensemble for synthesis prediction (Yale, Nature 2026)

I proposed the LoRA post-training strategy, and co-implemented it, that specialized an ensemble of 2,489 domain-expert LLMs to turn the reaction literature into executable synthesis protocols.

Billion-scale virtual screening (Yale)

An in-house, ML-accelerated screening platform from SMILES to hit-enriched candidates. Against GSK3β it surfaced three hits with novel scaffolds; those results were adopted into a proposal that won a Yale Ventures Blavatnik award.

Working with partner programs

What a partner receives, on a standing cadence:

  • Ranked design packages with structural rationale and per-residue SAR, not raw scores.
  • FEP maps with edges and poses flagged for human adjudication, so the chemists know where to look.
  • Target assessments covering druggability, binding-site tractability, and the prior-art and patent landscape.
  • Candidates carried through to experimental follow-up, with the computational data delivered alongside.
